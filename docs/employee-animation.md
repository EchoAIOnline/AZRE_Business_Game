# Employee animation

The four existing Microsoft Rocketbox character GLBs, textures and materials are unchanged. They use Bip01 skeletons (81 joints, with finger and eye bones), not Mixamo bone names. A Mixamo FBX would require humanoid bone mapping, rest-pose/axis correction, root-motion conversion and baking. The original Rocketbox animation library already uses the compatible skeleton; this upgrade uses its authored clips instead of regenerating a procedural walk. Their individual motion-capture provenance has not been verified.

Each employee has four animation-only GLBs: `Standing` (`idle_neutral_01`), `Walking` (`walk_slow_01`), `StandUp` (`sit_stand_up_chair_01`), and `SitDown` (`sit_down_chair_01`). Male and female sources are separately baked onto each original rig. `scripts/bake-employee-animations.py` records the Blender process. The character assets are never re-exported. Sources and license are recorded in `public/models/ATTRIBUTION.md`.

`employeeClips.ts` loads each pack once, maps normalized bone names, and retains captured bone motion. Navigation owns horizontal travel: walking root displacement is removed and its measured speed sets mixer playback speed to match the 0.65 m/s controller movement. Vertical and lateral sway remain. Loop endpoints are blended over 180 ms. Chair clips are one-shots, use their actual durations, and fade at both ends. Horizontal chair root travel is suppressed to keep the employee aligned with their assigned workstation; this is root-motion handling, not a full foot-contact IK solver. Some foot drift during chair transitions remains possible.

The existing seated breathing clip is retained. Desk-specific arm IK is still necessary because the library has no verified keyboard/mouse clip matching these desks. Wrist movement is reduced, fingers tap independently with different intervals, and the mouse hand stops typing. Existing contact clearance checks remain. Head and eye movement stay subtle. Typing is procedural contact animation, not motion capture.

The seven visual states remain IDLE, WORKING, WALKING, COLLABORATING, TASK_COMPLETED, AWAITING_APPROVAL and ERROR. Their physical phases remain seated, standing up, outbound walking, standing idle, returning, aligning and sitting down. Breaks start after 150–335 seconds and recur after roughly 180–398 seconds, independently randomized. The automatic work/rest schedule remains independent for each employee. Visual preview controls execute no tasks and change no CRM records.

Department boundaries, furniture/glass collision checks, other-employee and visitor avoidance, first-person controls, and avatar interactions remain intact. Employees turn toward the next path segment before stepping rather than translating sideways. They return to their home before sitting. Opening a modal or hiding the document pauses simulation. Asset skeletons are independent, while geometry/textures and decoded clip resources are reused.

Validation: TypeScript and production build; asset sharing/skeleton isolation; all four rigs playing authored standing, walking and one-shot chair clips; finite transforms and seamless walk loops; boundary/furniture/thin-wall routing, dynamic blocking, state transitions and return-home checks; existing first-person collision and work-schedule tests. Browser checks cover authored walking and return to seated work with no console errors. Visual review is still needed on the user's preferred viewpoints; this is not a claim of perfect biomechanical contact.
